[row, column]

So:

[3, 3]

means:

Row 3, Column 3

That's it.

FIRST number = ROW
SECOND number = COLUMN

Therefore:

[3, 3]
 ↑  ↑

Now comes the REALLY important part: numbers like -2 and -1

This is where things usually become confusing.

When we write:

[-2, -1]

we are NOT describing a square.

We're describing a change in position.

This is the key distinction.

Compare:

[3, 3]

with:

[-2, -1]

[3,3] says:

Where am I?

[-2,-1] says:

How should I move?

Think of it like this:

CURRENT POSITION
      ↓
   [3, 3]

MOVEMENT INSTRUCTION
      ↓
  [-2, -1]



| Movement  | Row    | Column  | Meaning |
| --------- | ------ | ------- | ------- |
| `[-2,-1]` | up 2   | left 1  | ↖[1, 2] |
| `[-2,+1]` | up 2   | right 1 | ↗[1, 4] |
| `[-1,-2]` | up 1   | left 2  | ↖[2, 1] |
| `[-1,+2]` | up 1   | right 2 | ↗[2, 5] |
| `[+1,-2]` | down 1 | left 2  | ↙[4, 1] |
| `[+1,+2]` | down 1 | right 2 | ↘[4, 5] |
| `[+2,-1]` | down 2 | left 1  | ↙[5, 2] |
| `[+2,+1]` | down 2 | right 1 | ↘[5, 4] |

Moving UP means subtracting from the row.

So:

-1 = up 1 row
-2 = up 2 rows


Moving DOWN means adding to the row.

So:

+1 = down 1 row
+2 = down 2 rows

ROW MOVEMENT

-2 → up 2
-1 → up 1
+1 → down 1
+2 → down 2

COLUMN MOVEMENT

-2 → left 2
-1 → left 1
+1 → right 1
+2 → right 2

Notice something beautiful:

There are really only two L-shapes:

2 rows + 1 column

and:

1 row + 2 columns

Then we flip those L-shapes around in all directions.

That's how we get 8.

Places where each move meets on the board 

                 [1,2]   [1,4]


          [2,1]             [2,5]

                   [3,3]


          [4,1]             [4,5]


                 [5,2]   [5,4]



16. But wait — are all eight ALWAYS possible?

No!

This is one of the most important ideas for your Knights Travails project.

The knight always has eight possible movement patterns.

But from a particular square, some of those patterns may take the knight outside the board.

That's what you meant when you said:

some are valid while others aren't.

Exactly.

                 A graph has:

VERTICES (nodes)

On our chessboard:

EVERY SQUARE = A NODE
